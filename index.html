    How this model works, and its limits

    This is a simplified projection, not an official one. It starts from the 2026 Trustees Report intermediate path (combined reserves depleted in 2034; 75-year deficit of 4.42% of payroll) and applies each lever as a rule of thumb. Levers for taxes, retirement age and the cost-of-living adjustment are tuned to land near the published single-option scores above.

    Levers tagged rough (disability, survivors, minimum benefit, caregiver credits, life expectancy, wages, employment, interest rates, recessions and market crashes) have no published score behind them here; they are order-of-magnitude sensitivities. Disability and survivor benefits are modeled as about 10% and 13% of total cost.

    The model covers the combined OASDI funds; the retirement fund (OASI) alone runs dry earlier, in 2032. Equity investing is shown at its expected return, with crashes only if you add one in Shocks. Treasury deposits ignore the borrowing cost. Preset proposals are loose translations of their main provisions.
